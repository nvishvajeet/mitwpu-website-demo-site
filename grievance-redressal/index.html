Raising a concern

Grievance redressal

Any student can raise a grievance by writing to grc@mitwpu.edu.in, or through the online grievance form on the Students Grievance Redressal Committee’s page. Ragging, sexual harassment and caste discrimination each have a committee of their own, with its own address — the cards below say which body handles what, in the words you would use for the problem rather than the words the committee uses for itself.

Grievance redressal directory

Raise a grievance

Write to grc@mitwpu.edu.in, submit the online grievance form, or hand a letter to the Chairperson of the Students Grievance Redressal Committee. The committee records and verifies the complaint, fixes a date to hear it, and you may appear in person or send a representative. The university’s Student Query Resolution Framework sets out the earlier steps — your mentor first, then escalation to the Registrar’s Office — for anything that has not yet become a formal grievance.

Students Grievance Redressal Committee (SGRC)→Student Query Resolution Framework (PDF)→UGC e-Samadhan portal↗

If the committee does not resolve it

A grievance the Students Grievance Redressal Committee has not resolved within the period the UGC (Redressal of Grievances of Students) Regulations, 2023 allow may be referred to the Ombudsperson, who hears both sides and passes an order the institution is required to comply with. Students may also lodge a grievance directly on the UGC’s national e-Samadhan portal.

Referral to the Ombudsperson: the published procedure→UGC e-Samadhan portal↗

Ragging, bullying and intimidation

The Anti-Ragging Committee is constituted under the Maharashtra Prohibition of Ragging Act 1999, the UGC regulation on curbing the menace of ragging, and the corresponding AICTE notification. It enforces a zero-tolerance policy on ragging, bullying, intimidation, coercion and harassment, and acts on reported incidents. Students and employees can write to argc@mitwpu.edu.in. An Anti-Ragging Squad and named nodal officers operate alongside it.

Anti-Ragging Committee→National Ragging Prevention Programme→Anti-Ragging Committee 2025–2027 (PDF)→Anti-Ragging Squad (PDF)→UGC anti-ragging regulations (Annexure I)↗

Sexual harassment

The Internal Complaints Committee is constituted under the UGC (Prevention, prohibition and redressal of sexual harassment of women employees and students in higher educational institutions) Regulations, 2015. Students and employees can write to icc@mitwpu.edu.in. The university also describes a Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) Committee in its equality and diversity policy; it does not publish a page for it, and does not say whether the two are the same body.

Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)→Internal Complaints Committee constitution (PDF)→Equality and diversity policy, including the POSH Committee→

Caste-based discrimination

The SC/ST Cell and Equal Opportunity Cell was constituted under the UGC guidelines on prevention of caste-based discrimination in higher education institutions. It investigates incidents and acts on them, and undertakes to keep the process confidential and impartial. Complainants from SC, ST and OBC categories can write to scstc@mitwpu.edu.in or use the complaint form on the cell’s page.

SC / ST Cell and Equal Opportunity Cell→Policy for promoting equality and diversity→Equality and diversity policy (PDF)→

Conduct and discipline

The Disciplinary Committee is constituted under Section 8(3) of the University’s First Ordinance. It investigates violations of the code of conduct, including academic misconduct and behavioural issues, and decides what disciplinary action follows. This is the body a complaint about another student’s conduct reaches, rather than one a student brings a grievance to.

Disciplinary Committee→University Discipline Committee 2025 (PDF)→Students’ Code of Conduct (PDF)→

Academic and examination concerns

Questions about assessment, transcripts, grade cards and certificates go to the examination helpdesks before they become a grievance, and the ordinances are what those decisions are made under. What the helpdesks cannot settle returns to the Students Grievance Redressal Committee.

Student assistance and helpdesks→Ordinances and regulations→Student resources and handbooks→

Support while a complaint is open

Raising a grievance is not the only thing a student in difficulty needs. Aakriti provides counselling and crisis support; the Centre for Women’s Studies and Development runs counselling, advocacy and safety audits; the students’ welfare office is the general front door.

Aakriti — Centre for Holistic Well-Being→Centre for Women’s Studies and Development→Students’ Welfare→

Employees

Staff grievances go to the Employee Grievance Redressal Committee, constituted under Clause 30(2) of the University Statutes to give every employee a route for redressal and to prevent unfair practice. The Anti-Ragging Committee and the Internal Complaints Committee take complaints from employees as well as students.
