University services

Computing Resources Centre

The network, your accounts, your email, the software you may install and the machines your research runs on — and, said plainly, the parts of that the university has not yet decided.

Before you start

The Centre is a proposal, and most of these answers are missing

No unit of this name appears anywhere the university publishes, and no computing office appears in its directory of administrative offices. The name is a proposal; the service it describes is real work somebody does, and the pages here are what that work has to publish before anybody can use it.

So an answer here is one of three things: a fact read off a page the university published, with the address it was read from beside it; a question nobody has answered, kept and marked as such; or a sample, marked as a sample, showing the shape of an instruction the real version will replace. Nothing on these pages is a plausible guess, and that is the only guarantee they offer.

What the Centre is answerable for

Everything a member of this university touches that is not paper. Six areas, and they are listed here rather than left implied because a service nobody has been made answerable for is a service that is nobody's to fix at four o'clock on a Friday.

The network
Wired and wireless on campus, the connection out to the internet, and — when it exists — the route back in from outside it.
Accounts and identity
One sign-in per person: when it starts working, what it opens, and what happens to it when the person leaves.
Email
The address, where it is read, and how long it lasts after graduation or resignation.
Software and licensing
What each kind of member may install, at whose cost, and what happens to the licence when they go.
Research computing
Machines larger than a workstation, whether the university's own or reached remotely at a national facility.
Security
Phishing, compromised accounts, what to do first, and who to tell.

Your own laptop, and the rooms you teach in

The machine you are required to own, and the equipment you are not.

  • What device do I need, and what happens if it fails in an examination?

    You sit examinations on your own device, so this is not an optional purchase. The university publishes what the device has to do rather than what to buy: a working camera and microphone, a supported operating system, a charge that outlasts the paper, a data connection as a fallback, and a power bank.

    It says wireless is provided for digital examinations, and that queries during one go to the technical support centre or the online proctor.

    No minimum specification, no recommended model, no loan scheme and no hardship provision is published. A student who cannot afford a suitable device has nowhere on this site to turn.

    Who owns this: Examination Helpdesk. The Department of Examinations' public helpdesk. It is the only support service on the university's website that publishes a telephone number, an email address, a room and its opening hours together.
