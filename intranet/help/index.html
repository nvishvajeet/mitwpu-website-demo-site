University services

Report a problem, request a service

What to do when something is broken, something is missing, or something has gone wrong — and who owns the answer.

Before you start

Look for the answer here first

Most of what people ring somebody about is written down. Read the answer below before you raise anything with anyone: the best request is the one nobody needed to make, and the teams here are small.

If you do need to ask, there is no single service desk. The university does not publish one number for everything, so each service is reached separately and the routes below are the ones it publishes itself.

Where it has published nothing, this page says so. It does not guess. Those gaps are collected at the foot of the page.

This page takes no requests itself. It has no form on it and will not have one: it names the system that handles a thing and sends you there.

How to use this page

Look for the thing that has gone wrong, not for the department that owns it. The owner is named inside the answer, because you should not have to know the organisation chart to report a broken air conditioner.

Every telephone number, email address, room and opening time below is one the university publishes, shown with the page it came from. Nothing has been filled in from memory, from another university, or from what seemed likely.

These answers say what a service is and who runs it. Step-by-step instructions — how to configure a client, what a particular failure looks like — belong on the pages behind each service, and are a different kind of writing from this one.

Something is broken

A fault in a room, a building or a piece of equipment.

  • Something is broken in a teaching room, a laboratory or an office

    Email the Office of Infra & Facilities. Say which building, which floor and which room, and what is wrong with it.

    This is the only route the university publishes for a fault anywhere on campus. There is no fault-reporting system, no maintenance number and no counter to walk up to.

    Who owns this: Office of Infra & Facilities. The office named for campus infrastructure and facilities in the university's own directory of administrative offices.

    The office is listed as "Office of Infra & Facilities" in the university's directory of administrative offices, which gives one email address and no telephone number. Published at /about-us/administrative-offices

    • Administrative offices, with an email for each
  • Something is broken in my hostel room

    Tell the warden or the student support staff at your hostel straight away, and email the hostel administration if it is not fixed.

    Report it early. The hostel rules say a resident pays for repairs or replacement of what they damage, and a fault left unreported can become one of those.

    Who owns this: Hostel administration. The team that runs the residences at Ambegaon, Bavdhan, Karvenagar and Kothrud, their mess, their laundry and the shuttle between them and the campus.

    • Request system no request system is published
    • Telephone 020-48552855
    • Email hostel.admin@mitwpu.edu.in
    • Where they are office location not yet published here
    • Hours opening hours not yet published here

    The hostel's own answer to damaged property is that students should immediately inform the warden or student support staff, and that students must bear the cost of repairs or replacement. Published at /life-wpu/hostel-facilities

    The university has not published who to tell. Housekeeping is described as a service the residences provide, but no team, address or number is given for it anywhere.

If something has gone seriously wrong

Complaints, harassment, ragging and discrimination are not service requests, and they have their own statutory routes.

  • I need to make a formal complaint

    Do not use anything on this page. The university has statutory committees for grievances, ragging, harassment and discrimination, each with its own address and its own procedure, and each is the correct and protected route for what it covers.

    They are listed in the footer of every page on this site and are not restated here: a second, differently worded route to a statutory committee is worse than one.

Ask the Computing Resources Centre

The questions below are the ones a computing service is asked, in the words people actually use. Read them before writing to anyone; several have an answer already, and the ones that do not are marked plainly rather than filled in with something plausible.

An unanswered question here is not an oversight. It is a decision the university has still to take and publish, and it is on the work list at the foot of this page so that somebody can take it.

No unit of this name appears anywhere the university publishes, and no IT office appears in its directory of administrative offices. The name is a proposal, and it follows the Indian convention — IIT Bombay and IIT Kanpur have a Computer Centre, IIT Delhi a Computer Services Centre — so that a colleague arriving from another institution recognises it rather than has to learn it. "Centre" matches the university's own usage: it writes Knowledge Resource Centre, Centre for Holistic Well-Being and Centres of Excellence.
