Pay, leave and your appointment

The employee half of the intranet: what the university publishes about pay, leave, your appointment, appraisal, benefits and grievance — and, in plain sight, what it does not.

Start here

This page publishes; HROne transacts

Applying for leave, seeing a payslip and keeping your own record are HROne's job — the university runs it and it needs an institutional sign-in. This page is the map: what each service is, who owns it, and where the university has published nothing.

Like every page of this site it hosts no form and takes no personal detail. Nothing here is a substitute for your letter of appointment; where the two differ, the letter is the university's word and this page is not.

How to use this page

Questions are grouped by what you are trying to do, not by which office owns it, because an employee arrives with a problem rather than with a department. The owning team is named inside each answer.

An answer marked Not published is a question the university has not answered anywhere a person can read. It is kept, and it is counted at the foot of this page, because a well-chosen question is a specification for whoever fills it in.

Raising a grievance as an employee

Where a complaint about your employment goes, and where harassment goes.

  • I have a grievance about my employment

    The Employee Grievance Redressal Committee is the body for this. It is constituted under Clause 30(2) of the University Statutes, and its published constitution states its purpose plainly: to ensure transparency, to prevent unfair practices, and to provide a mechanism to all employees for the redressal of their grievances.

    The committee's membership is published in that document — it is chaired by the Vice-Chancellor and includes the Director of HR and the Registrar. What is not published is how you reach it: no address, no form, no timetable and no statement of what happens after a grievance is submitted.

    Who owns this: Employee Grievance Redressal Committee. Constituted under Clause 30(2) of the University Statutes to ensure transparency, prevent unfair practice, and give every employee a mechanism for the redressal of grievances.

The employment documents the university does not publish

Not a wish list. Each of these is a document an employee is entitled to be able to read, which nothing on the university's site links to, and each is the reason one or more questions above is unanswered.

  • Employee service rules, or a staff handbook — conditions of service, leave, promotion and benefits in one place; nothing on the university's site links to such a document /faculty-staff
  • An employee code of conduct — the student code of conduct is published and there is no counterpart for staff; the student one was deliberately not relabelled /faculty-staff
  • A leave policy — no entitlement, notice period or approval route for any kind of leave /about-us/administrative-offices
  • A payroll calendar — no pay date, pay cycle or payslip route /about-us/administrative-offices
  • An appraisal and promotion scheme — no cycle, criteria, reviewer or progression route for teaching or professional staff /about-us/administrative-offices
  • A provident fund and gratuity note — no establishment reference, no route to a UAN, nothing on transferring a fund in /about-us/administrative-offices
  • An onboarding checklist — nothing on which accounts a new employee gets, who creates them, or what to bring on the first day /about-us/administrative-offices
  • An exit and clearance process — no notice period, clearance, exit interview or final-settlement timetable /about-us/administrative-offices
  • A route to the Employee Grievance Redressal Committee — the committee and its membership are published; how an employee submits a grievance to it is not /student-information
  • A recruitment and establishment procedure — how a post is created, approved and released for advertisement, which is the establishment half of the HR office's own name /about-us/administrative-offices
